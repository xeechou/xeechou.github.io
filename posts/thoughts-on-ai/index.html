A few thoughts on AI

29 May 2026

Initially I plan to write this post in 2024, that was in the era of GPT-4 and Claude 3.5. The original titled "reflection on career in age or AI". I figured if there is anything opinion was written then it would look pretty stupid by now. We were in the hype of prompt engineering, now went through MCP/tool use. Now it is skills and agentic AIs. I do not know if at this point any conclusions can be drawn to test against a few month. But anyway, for pure entertainment purpose, cannot worry to much not embarrassing myself in the future.

Disclaimer: Any opinions are purely my own, does not associate with my employer or anyone colleague. The opinion is purely for personal use only I do not give or will give any suggestions on how you should apply AI to your profession work.

Programmer's point of view

I suppose it is safe to say at least for now, software engineering is the forefront of the AI applications. While most people are chatting with OpenAI in the browser, before OpenClaw came into public, we already have agentic tools like github copilot, cursor now the cool kid in town, Claude code and codex. I am in a very lucky position to have some of them at my disposal, being able to use the frontier models daily without need to worry too much about the cost. if you use them, programming activity is a lot different with/without.

The good

Before, giving a large code base to work on, getting familiar with it takes quite a while. Before you get the hang of it, feels more like sailing on a foggy day (how would I know, I never sailed in fog). Tools and documentations are your only friend to navigate your way around. Looking some sample code somewhere, get confused, maybe stuck on some quirks which is not related.

Right now, with LLM tools, you just ask questions, tell it to generate SVGs or the software architectures, ask it to generate sample code for getting started. What's daunting before feels more like a small exercise, give you some "blind" confidence.

Taking it further, you can ask it to pair programming with you, brain storm with LLMs, generate plans. Scaffolding the code, writing test cases, a lot of tedious can be skipped entirely. Some of which are error prone for human, we make stupid mistakes, then spending 2 days debugging it.

Or even further, like some top developers from Spotify 1 which is stop writing code completely. Only prompting the AI to do it.

Your mileage may vary depends on your personality, how confident you are with AI generated code, etc. One thing is certain, the code itself is certainly much cheaper to generate than before. It almost like writing essays, or even simpler than that (because LLMs will generate something no matter what), as long as you can type, maybe even not you can just speak, regardless of the quality, there will be something generated at the end.

When generated code does not compile, the tools like cursor can iterate, feed the output from compiler back to the context and again, then again, then again…

Same thing can be said for the debugging/testing now, just because pure software has this really smooth feedback loop.

This is indeed a huge step forward from ChatGPT came out. People stop asking if it works, why it works, how it works or why it does not work since we all got used to chat with LLMs. How many people are taking AI chat-bots as auto-regressive token predictions model (which is what they are indeed) when interact with it? It is amazed how fast we are adapting.

The bad

Then we may immediately start arguing software is dead. Open source is no longer valid. Every 6 months, we see predictions from AI company that 90-100 percent of software will be written by AI 2. Will we all lose our jobs? Software written by AI used by AI I suppose, rest of us will just play with stones then starve to death? I suppose we will see, so far I am still writing code.

On the other hand, LLMs are known to hallucinate, a phenomenon some refer to as "stochastic parrots"3. Amazon is currently enforcing senior developers to sign off on generated code4. Some of us, at least, have doubts about the quality and security of such code. Thanks to AI tools, we are in the midst of a huge junior hiring freeze5. HR departments may be worrying that no one will be left to fix the AI-generated code once the senior developers retire.

I am not going to try answering these big questions here — that is way above my pay grade. My concerns are more practical: dealing with AI-generated code on a daily basis.

I have found that reviewing AI-generated code takes a different kind of effort than writing prompts. When you are prompting, there is a creative momentum to it. You describe what you want, iterate on the output, and watch something take shape. Reviewing the generated code is a totally different picture.

With current approach many people using AI, which is "if it runs then merge it", reviewing the generated code is not fun at all. There is generally not a main thread you can follow, see how the changes takes shape. It became a giant brain dump. Inconsistent styles and abstractions. There are even cases the authors didn't even understand what code is before they submit the PRs 6.

The reviews are the real bottleneck, if you care about the quality of the code, you might find yourself on the opposite side of the team, if your team wants to ship features quickly. There are new demos to prepare, stakeholders to impress, and a pace of development that keeps accelerating.

I am not saying that you couldn't write quality code using AI at all. You can effectively can, help you brain-storming, PoC and fail fast, find your design flaws early on. You can even generate multiple drafts with agents then learn something from each one of them. But then this is optimizing for different things and potentially really costly.

The ugly

Now I am attempting to touch a bit on the topic "if software is dead" question, Dario would tell you 100%, since he wins all the way in this scenario. It probably gives many people anxieties in the past a few years because he has to reminds you every 6 months about it.

I do not know if this question can be answered at the economy perspective. Maybe it is much cheaper to generate the code (although at the time of writing it does not look like this way), I can only go ask, what is code exactly? This artifact, human thoughts organized into type checked context free grammars. You might be expressing the algorithm, describing an abstract structure. Pieces of nodes that can work together, not just human can understand it, compiler can as well. Eventually with compiler and linker, the machine can understand it as well. So it is a way human expressing their ideas, much like writing a essay, a song, a painting.

You maybe asking, it is art then? We should frame it in a picture and auction the code in a hall? On the other end of it, code being compiled becomes libraries, software, applications, where it is useful to assist people finishing some tasks, so in this sense it is different than the traditional sense of art, the economic value of code comes from its usefulness. This is the part where being attacked by AI generation the most.

Still, I do not think "software is dead", just as pure economic sense, you wouldn't want to resolving the same problem then forget about the solutions every time. As a comparison, it maybe equivalent to rediscover how to make fire every time you need it, or rediscover the earth is orbiting the sun every day. We do not do that right? We summarize our knowledge and build on top of it, we might update our knowledge/techniques if there is better way doing things but starting from scratch every-time would have us stagnate and never advance anymore. So yes, I don't think "software is dead" as long as there are new things to be learned.

Outro

We cannot go back in time, the genie is out of the bottle now this world is forever changed. I missed the old days there is no way you can cheat when writing code, have to do it line by line, right now it takes great will power not to fall into vibe coding mode, which is not good for your mental health and ability to think. On the other hand, the LLMs at its current form (not AGI) is indeed a very powerful tool. Think about this way, vast human knowledge is immediately available to you, no longer need to going through sea of articles to find the information you need, now you just ask.

Except of course the bills is way too high right now. For the AI to be able to help everyone, not just top elites, local-ai is the only way forward. Not just it is cheaper, but I think also for it to reach true pervasiveness.
